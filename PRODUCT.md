# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Architecture studios, recruiters, and prospective clients visit the portfolio to quickly understand Vanessa Benipayo's point of view, project experience, technical range, and availability. They may be scanning on a laptop during a hiring review or on a phone after a referral. The site must make the work legible before asking for contact.

## Product Purpose

This is a working portfolio for an architect at the beginning of practice. It turns selected projects, thesis work, technical capabilities, and process into evidence of judgment. Success is a visitor understanding the kind of architect Vanessa is and taking a confident next step: reviewing a project, saving the contact, or starting a conversation.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, exacting, and materially grounded. The voice is observant and direct, with the confidence of a well-made drawing set rather than the volume of a sales page. Architecture is presented as a practice of attention to place, people, proportion, and buildability.

## Anti-references

Do not look like a generic architect portfolio template, a SaaS landing page, or a fashion-editorial mood board. Avoid dark-tech styling, purple gradients, neon accents, glassmorphism, oversized decorative UI, repeated feature cards, and auto-rotating hero carousels that compete with the work. Avoid architectural clichés such as vague claims about "elevating spaces," empty luxury language, and image galleries with no project context. The editorial direction should feel like a working dossier, not a magazine imitation.

## Design Principles

1. **Show process before promise.** Drawings, decisions, constraints, and outcomes carry more trust than abstract claims.
2. **Treat the index like a drawing set.** Projects should be ordered, scannable, and context-rich rather than arranged as a wall of identical cards.
3. **Keep place in the frame.** Every project should expose its location, role, year, scale, and status before the viewer opens it.
4. **Use motion as wayfinding.** Movement should clarify hierarchy and depth, never decorate the page; honor reduced-motion preferences by default.
5. **Make the next step legible.** Contact should feel like a natural continuation of the work, with clear information for both hiring conversations and client inquiries.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Maintain keyboard access and visible focus states, semantic landmarks, descriptive image alternatives, readable line lengths, and contrast that does not rely on color alone. Provide touch targets of at least 44px, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and do not auto-advance or auto-animate content for people who request reduced motion. Use direct, non-exclusive language and keep project information understandable without specialist knowledge.
